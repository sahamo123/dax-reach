# Conversion and UX

Adapted from Corey Haines' CRO workflow; technical and content constraints are integrated here.

Identify the page type, traffic source, primary action, audience readiness, and what constitutes a qualified conversion. Inspect the actual desktop and mobile journey if available. A screenshot supports visual observations, not form-delivery claims.

Prioritize clarity of the offer, relevance of the headline, CTA hierarchy, scannability, credible proof, objections, and interaction friction. Put proof near the claim it supports. Separate service buyers and learners with explicit destinations. Link to useful internal detail pages rather than forcing every answer onto the homepage.

For forms, justify requested fields, provide labels and useful errors, preserve keyboard access and focus, and explain what happens after submission. Test validation and success behavior locally. Only send a live test submission when authorized; label it and confirm the actual downstream result where access permits.

For motion, prefer existing lightweight patterns. Keep meaningful copy and links available without animation. Test reduced motion, keyboard use, small screens, zoom, loading behavior, and layout stability. Do not hide the primary explanation behind hover, autoplay, or a scroll effect. Animation quality and SEO performance require distinct checks.

Use truthful testimonials, real credentials, substantiated security practices, and clear service scope. Avoid fabricated badges, scarcity, client counts, endorsements, or unsupported claims about competitors. Access-control and training details are more informative than an unverified "bank-level security" label.

Separate bug fixes from experiments. For an experiment, specify hypothesis, variant, primary metric, guardrails, audience, and stopping plan. Low traffic may support qualitative checks and a monitored change before a statistically meaningful A/B test. Report uncertainty rather than declaring a winner from a few leads.

Deliver prioritized changes with observable problems and expected mechanisms, finished copy when requested, and a verification plan. Preserve accessibility, essential content, and functional navigation while simplifying the layout.
