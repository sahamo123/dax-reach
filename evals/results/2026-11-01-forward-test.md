## Request 3 — Practical article

**Proposed title:** How to Organize Records Before Your Monthly Bookkeeping Review

**Proposed description:** Use a fictional company's example to gather records, flag missing documents, and prepare clear questions before a monthly bookkeeping review.

### Article draft

A monthly bookkeeping review starts with records that are easy to find and questions that are easy to answer. The following example uses fictional company Harbor Workshop; its records and situations are invented.

First, create a folder for the review month. Inside it, separate bank statements, card statements, customer invoices, vendor bills, receipts, and questions. Keep original files, and use consistent names such as “October_vendor_invoice_1042.pdf.” Avoid storing multiple copies under different names.

Next, gather statements for every business account used during the month. Include all pages, even when a page appears blank. Add supporting documents for transfers, refunds, loans, or other activity your bookkeeper may need to understand.

Then match supporting documents to the transactions they explain. At fictional Harbor Workshop, a card charge labeled “Supply Depot” needs its receipt and a note identifying the project. If a document is missing, record the date, amount, supplier, and person responsible for finding it. Do not invent the missing details.

Create a questions list before the meeting. Flag unfamiliar charges, possible duplicates, unpaid invoices, and payments whose purpose is unclear. Keep questions separate from confirmed facts so the reviewer can investigate without guessing.

Finally, share the folder through your agreed document channel and check that your bookkeeper can open the files. During the review, assign an owner and next step to each unresolved item. Carry those items forward until they are resolved.

For help building a repeatable monthly routine, explore our [bookkeeping services](/services/bookkeeping/) and discuss the records your business needs to prepare.
