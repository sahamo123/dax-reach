# Strategy

Adapted from Corey Haines' product-marketing and content-strategy workflows; see NOTICE.

Start with the decision the visitor needs to make, their buying stage, and the business outcome. Separate audiences when their needs diverge: a prospective buyer and an existing customer seeking training need distinct destinations and CTAs. A homepage may route both without describing every product, curriculum, or service detail.

Use approved offer facts and actual customer language. Map each audience to problem, offer, evidence, objections, and next action. Distinguish direct competitors, alternative methods, and doing nothing. Do not infer competitors' weaknesses from missing website claims.

Inventory existing pages, their intended query/intent, incoming links, observed performance, and destination CTA. Preserve useful performing pages; improving a site does not require rewriting everything. Consolidate overlapping pages only with evidence and a redirect/link plan. Identify public service pages that need crawlable internal links.

Choose content topics by relevance to a customer decision, the team's first-hand contribution, observed demand when data exists, and ability to deliver value. Label keyword volume unknown when no source provides it. For each topic, explain what the page adds beyond existing results: a real process example, demonstrated comparison, original evidence, or useful tool. Avoid creating pages for every keyword variant.

Output: audience-to-page map, proposed page hierarchy, source-backed positioning, and a small prioritized backlog. Each item should name its visitor benefit, evidence needed, intended next action, and measurement. A priority ranking is editorial judgment, not a Google metric.

Do not turn this package into a mandatory replacement for the user's existing brand context. Resolve material offer conflicts; retain approved terminology and voice.
