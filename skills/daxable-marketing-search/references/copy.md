# Copy and Editing

Adapted from Corey Haines' copywriting workflow. Its upstream file credits Profound's improved-copywriting skill for parts of its drafting method; attribution is retained in the source snapshot and NOTICE.

Determine whether this is a new draft, improvement of a recognizable voice, or a narrow edit. Preserve the user's angle and scope. Read approved audience, offer, proof, objections, and voice. A point edit should not trigger a page rewrite.

Write the visitor outcome first, connect it to a credible mechanism, and show the next step. Distinguish feature from benefit: organizing transactions is the work; understanding cash flow and having less bookkeeping to handle are visitor benefits. Quantify savings only from evidence. Avoid promises such as guaranteed tax readiness when the scope or records do not support them.

For a page, organize the argument into clear units: headline, supporting explanation, benefits, relevant proof, offer/process, objections, and CTA. Use only the units the page needs. Draft a few alternatives for the key decision points, choose one with a reason, and assemble the final copy. Keep the majority plain and specific.

Editing pass: remove vague claims, repetitions, jargon, inflated urgency, decorative statistics, and unnecessary adjectives. Keep sentences easy to scan and headings meaningful. Respect the brand's casing rules. Preserve useful detail through contextual links or accessible expandable content rather than deleting the answer solely to shorten the page.

Claims review: for credentials, locations, insurance, partnerships, service coverage, prices, and reviews, use approved facts with sources. If a material claim lacks evidence, write a supportable alternative and state exactly what would substantiate the stronger version. Competitor comparisons need a fair definition and current evidence.

Deliver ready-to-use copy, optional short alternatives, and a brief explanation of the main change. A different phrasing is not automatically better; retain good existing copy unless there is a concrete problem to solve.
