# Content: Brief, Draft, or Refresh

Combines Corey's intent and differentiation approach with AgriciDaniel's content-quality review. See NOTICE and Google rules G02, G03, G04.

## Brief

Identify the intended reader, their question, buying stage, target page, and useful next action. Inspect current search results when available; record locale and date. A result summary is context, not permission to reproduce competitors' expression. Plan a distinct contribution from actual expertise, evidence, or a clearly labeled illustration.

Provide: working title, reader promise, scope, outline, primary sources, available first-hand material, internal destinations, CTA, and facts still needed. Choose length from what the task requires. Do not enforce word counts, keyword density, fixed answer-block length, or link quotas.

## Draft

Write a clear answer early, then explain the relevant choices, practical steps, limitations, and evidence. Use headings and comparisons where they help readers. Cite factual claims near the statement. Label fictional examples; never present them as client cases. Use authorship, review, and creation-method information when readers would reasonably expect it.

For financial, legal, health, or other high-impact advice, verify current primary sources and flag claims needing qualified review. A bookkeeping service page must not quietly promise tax advice, CPA credentials, or tax filing based on a hypothetical partnership. Generic writing instructions do not qualify the model as a licensed professional.

Deliver the draft, proposed title/description, contextual internal links, and any schema proposal separately. Keep material claims aligned across page text, metadata, and structured data. Do not add fake statistics to make a paragraph seem more citable.

## Refresh

First confirm the decline or outdated information with evidence. Separate seasonality, tracking failures, demand shifts, technical changes, and content relevance. Preserve useful text, URL identity, author history, and existing conversion paths. Make the smallest changes that solve demonstrated gaps. Change modification dates only for real updates; preserve the original publication date.

## Review

Check usefulness, factual accuracy, completeness for the question, distinctive contribution, source quality, reading effort, and next action. Report gaps directly; do not invent a Google E-E-A-T score or rank guarantee. Produce finished copy when authorized, not only an audit checklist.
