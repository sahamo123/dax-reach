# Measurement and Diagnosis

For falling leads, examine the full sequence: demand, visibility, visits, page engagement, CTA use, form delivery, lead qualification, and follow-up. Verify tracking and form delivery before attributing the decline to copy, a photo, or an algorithm update.

Use comparable date windows, geography, device, channel, page, and offer. Record changes, seasonality, campaign shifts, and sample sizes. A before/after association does not establish a cause. At low volume, report uncertainty and avoid extrapolating a weekly count into a reliable conversion trend.

Use available Search Console exports, analytics, referral data, CRM outcomes, and real prompt observations. See Google G12 for its AI impressions report. Confirm which fields the actual report contains; do not report AI-specific clicks or leads from an impressions-only report. Preserve unknown/suppressed values even if a downloaded export encodes them as zero. Referrer data can be incomplete.

For AI prompt samples, define the audience, question categories, engines, dates, locale, and repetitions. Report the denominator and distinguish mentions, citations, and recommendations. Count only the tested prompt set. A local readiness review must not become an invented visibility percentage.

For the package itself, compare this version with upstream-only and unassisted baselines on the same task, artifacts, model, tools, and settings. Use blind output review when practical. Score factual correctness, useful completed work, evidence traceability, scope preservation, and unsupported claims. Record raw outputs and failures. Package lint and unit tests establish software properties, not copy quality or SEO lift.

For website outcomes, record the shipped change and subsequent qualified-lead/conversion observations. Choose an experiment suited to actual traffic. Retain the ability to reverse changes. Do not claim superiority until the relevant benchmark or outcome has been measured.
